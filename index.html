Before work begins

Sort the work into repair, replace or leave alone.

Take a property with outbuildings like a barn or shed. Each one may draw power through its own long cable run from the house, and whether that run is repaired or replaced can be decided separately from the equipment it feeds.

Write down each item you are unsure about and mark it as repair, replace or leave alone, with the reason beside it. A contractor can then confirm or change each decision in a written scope instead of a verbal opinion.

How do I know whether something needs repair or replacement?

Ask the contractor to name the observed fault, the likely cause and why repair would or would not hold. A written reason for each choice lets you compare quotes and see which items are optional.

Is it acceptable to leave a working item as it is?

Sometimes, if the contractor has assessed it and written down why it can stay. Leaving something alone should be a recorded decision with a stated reason, not an assumption that it is fine because it still works.

Can one written quote split urgent repairs from replacements I may postpone?

Yes, you can ask for each item to be listed on its own line with its reason. That way you can approve the repairs first and decide on the replacements later, once the reasons make sense to you.

What should I photograph around my Dunnville property first?

Photograph the panel door and its labels, the meter position and each outbuilding's visible outside wall or fixture, without removing any cover. Those photos help a contractor judge what to look at before anyone visits.

If I submit the form, does anyone come to repair my wiring?

No, the form brings nobody out. This site is for reading: each enquiry that arrives is reviewed, but the site sets up no visits, prices nothing and does no electrical work.
