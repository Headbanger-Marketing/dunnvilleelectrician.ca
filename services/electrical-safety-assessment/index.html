A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Dunnville property

Gather any receipts, notices or notes about earlier work, and write down which buildings and areas it touched as far as you know. Photograph visible equipment and labels from the outside, without removing covers.

Ask the contractor to say which findings are about condition, which are about the missing record and which items need no action. Ask also what the review cannot reach, such as wiring inside closed walls.

What should the assessment establish?

A report on undocumented earlier work at a Dunnville property that names faults, but not its purpose, scope or testing, leaves the owner unsure what was covered. So the report states its purpose, the equipment and buildings covered and whether testing was included. Each finding is marked for repair, replacement or no action, with a reason the homeowner can follow. The contractor explains what could not be seen and how that limits the conclusions. Ask whether the report suits an insurer or lender, if one requests it. It is never to be described as a complete check of everything on the property.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can earlier work with no records be judged by looking at it?

Not fully. Visible equipment can be reviewed, but concealed wiring and joins may stay unseen, so a contractor should state what was and was not examined and what that means for any decision to keep or replace.

Who should I ask for earlier records of work on a Dunnville property?

Ask the seller, a previous owner, the insurer or the lender whether anything was kept, and check invoices and ESA paperwork you hold. If nothing exists, the contractor can still record what is visible and note the gap.
