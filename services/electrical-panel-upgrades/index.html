A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Dunnville property

List the equipment you cannot leave unpowered for long, such as a sump pump or a freezer, and note which building each one is fed from. Photograph the closed panel door with its labels.

Ask the contractor which circuits will be off, for how long, and whether any outbuilding feed can be left connected. Ask also what existing parts would be reused, what would be replaced and what is being left alone.

What should the assessment establish?

Which parts of the supply equipment are sound, which need replacing and which can stay as they are? The scope answers part by part, and on a property with outbuildings the contractor gives the reason each existing feed to them is reused or replaced. If the utility is involved, who contacts it? The record names that party and states when the ESA notification for this Dunnville job is filed. Which circuits will be switched off, and in what order does the work proceed? The scope lists them in the order of the work, then describes how the supply is restored and checked.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the old panel stay if only one part of it is worn?

A contractor may recommend repairing or replacing one component and leaving the rest. Ask for that decision in writing, with the reason each remaining part can stay, rather than accepting a full replacement by default.

Who decides which buildings lose power during a service change?

You and the contractor decide together, because only you know which equipment cannot sit unpowered. Share that list early so the written plan can sequence the work and say which buildings are affected and when.
